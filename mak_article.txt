# MAK (1973–1978): The Lost Pioneers of Kosovar Rock

## The Band

MAK formed in Mitrovica in 1973 — the same year Bijelo Dugme formed in Sarajevo. Six members: Haki Misini (vocals), Mehmet Tupella, Rexhep Ismajli-Bulli, Halil Bajraktari, Sedat Isufi, and Xhevdet Gashi (guitar, main composer). Mixed Albanian and Serbian lineup, which was rare even then.

The name? Xhevdet Gashi revealed years later that MAK stood for "Muzicientët Ambicioz Kosovar" — Kosovar Ambitious Musicians. The public thought it referred to the poppy flower. The band never corrected anyone.

## The Music

Their sound was hard rock with Albanian lyrics — influenced by Deep Purple, The Beatles, and Jimi Hendrix. Their biggest hit, "Moj e mira te Pojata", was written by Gashi in a dorm room in Pristina in 1975 while he was a student. Recorded at Radio Television of Pristina in 1976. Debuted at the "Rinia këndon" festival to over 3,000 people. Stayed on top charts for weeks.

The song's musical inspiration: Deep Purple's "Black Night". Its lyrics: drawn from Albanian folk poetry.

**Known songs:**
1. Moj e mira te Pojata (1975/76) — their defining hit
2. Idila verore (Summer Idyll)
3. Tri gota (Three Glasses) — based on poetry by Latif Berisha
4. Dashuria e humbur (Lost Love)
5. Dil në derë o moj vogëlushe
6. Tito — a song about the Yugoslav president
7. Leskovik po fryn një erë
8. Në syrin tënd pash një lot
9. Qenke rritë moj s'të zen đemisha
10. Fryti i dashurisë — performed at Jugovizija 1984 in Skopje with Vera Oruqaj

MAK disbanded in 1978 after five years together. By then they had helped lay the foundation for Kosovar rock music — a scene that would explode through the 1980s with bands like Gjurmët, 403, Ilirët, Minatori, and the BOOM Festival.

## The War

Six members. Two didn't survive the 1990s.

Haki Misini — the band's voice. On June 10, 1999, hours before the NATO bombing of Yugoslavia ended, masked Serbian police in black uniforms came to his apartment in Mitrovica. His wife Bukurie watched them take him. His 11-year-old son Urim saw his father for the last time through a window — being loaded into a car with thirteen other people.

His body was found in 2005. In Suhodoll, a village near Mitrovica.

Mehmet Tupella was also killed during the Kosovo War.

## The Documentation

There is one book: "Grupi MAK (1973-1978) Prijës i muzikës rok në Kosovë" by Naim Kurteshi (2024, ISBN 9789951295130). 149 pages. In Albanian only. No English translation exists.

The recordings survive on YouTube as fan uploads — some with over 200,000 views. But there's no Wikipedia page for the band, no Discogs entry, no proper discography.

This is the gap I'm filling.

## Why This Matters

A band that formed the same year as Bijelo Dugme — a mixed Albanian-Serbian lineup in a divided city — that could have been remembered alongside the greats of Yugoslav rock. Instead, two of its members were killed in a war that the unlikely unity of their band should have transcended.

The songs are still there. The story isn't.
