Today, this voice comes to us only through songs that have remained archived...\n\nInsider\n\nMay 21, 2018 - 13:21 am\n\nClick Ketu to join Insider's YouTube channel.\n\nHaki Misini's voice is considered a hallmark of rock music in the 70s in Kosovo. Today, this voice comes to us only through the songs that have remained archived and that he performed as part of the group MAK, writes KultPlus.\n\nOn the tenth anniversary of Kosovo's Independence, KultPlus remembers some of the well-known figures of Kosovar art, whose artistic journey was interrupted during the last war in Kosovo, after they were killed by Serbian forces. Two members of the group MAK, Haki Misini and Mehmet Tupella, were killed during the last war of 1998-1999, leaving a huge void in Kosovar art. This rock group was formed under the influence of great world rock groups such as Deep Purple, The Beatles, and the well-known artist Jimy Hendrix. As a result, the new musical spirit of \"hard rock\" was born in Kosovo as well.\n\nHaki Misini's son, Urim Misini, then 11 years old, says in a documentary made by RTK that the last image he remembers of his father was three hours before the agreement for the arrival of NATO forces in Kosovo was signed. He says that he still remembers the loud knocks on the door and the noise in the apartment's hallway. Through the window, his last image was the crowd that took his father and other neighbors from his building.\n\nTwo policemen who did not look like that, since they were masked and in black clothes, had the appearance of criminals and as such come to the door of Haki Misini's apartment. The first contact with them was his wife, Bukurie Misini, whom they ask to call the owner of the house. The last thing that Haki Misini will take from his house are cigarettes with which he will leave so as not to return alive to his family.\n\n\"They asked Haki for his ID and green card, which they had given us during the war, and said, 'Get out.' When I started to speak, they said, 'Madam, close the door before it's too late.' Haki said, 'Can I just take the cigarettes?' I gave him the cigarettes and he shook my hand, he said, 'Come in,' because of the children's fear,\" his wife, Bukurie Misini, recounts. A car that had arrived near their building was filled with fourteen people in half an hour.\n\nBukurie Misini, through this documentary, remembers him as a calm and joyful person, she says that after every program he would return home, and in very important programs he would also invite her. Haki Misini's family, even after five years and after many attempts, did not manage to get any information about him. His body and several other people were found in 2005 in Suhodoll, Mitrovica, and after that his remains rest in the cemetery of the land of Kosovo. Haki Misini's voice is today immortalized in the song \"Moj e mira te pojata\", which is sung and interpreted by many other artists. His unique voice penetrates even today through the songs \"Tri gota\", \"Në syrin tënd pash një lot\", \"Idila verore\", \"Qenke rritë moj s'të zen đemisha\", and other songs.\n\nThe rock band MAK was formed in 1973 and functioned as a band until 1978. The six members of this band were: Haki Misini, Mehmet Tupella, Rexhep Ismajli-Bulli, Halil Bajraktari, Sedat Isufi and Xhevdet Gashi.
